Rx Lab app structure

How the iPhone and Watch apps should be organised for all your training: HYROX, CrossFit benchmarks and class WODs, Mayhem programming days, strength and Olympic lifting. This version answers two rounds of reviews and puts only race-critical work before Anaheim.

Every decision, with its reason, is in decisions.md in the same folder. All numbers in the mock screens are illustrative, except the Mayhem "previous result" and "recent maxes", which come from your own screenshots.

1What changed

Since draft 2 (review round 2)

  • Race flows use today's screens. Before Anaheim the target is set in the current Workouts tab and started from the watch's Workouts page; the new tabs come later.
  • Race plan is safer: shape from your best full sim (or a standard HYROX template), a "Delta off" switch, and the phone report shows target vs actual. New dates: test build Sun 15 Nov with a full trial sim, freeze Sun 22 Nov, then a half sim on the frozen build.
  • Crash recovery spelled out: Apple's workout recovery continues the same workout; result saved at the finish and at End with a revision number; a hidden "Simulate crash" button to rehearse.
  • Every barbell lift gets measured (squats, presses, later bench), in three steps: you mark each set's start and end now (Double Tap on the watch, "Set done" on the phone as backup); those marks become training data; then automatic set and rep detection, first as suggestions you confirm. See §7.
  • Strength blocks in a row run as one lifting block, but each lift keeps its own result. Suggested loads use maxes from the last 12 months, with the source shown.
  • No assumed division or sex: division and load column are a profile question with no preset.
  • Sync states are now ones the phone can actually know (the watch publishes its outbox list).
  • Plainer words, a Permissions page, distance run segments, IDs listed once in the build order.

Since draft 1

  • Before Anaheim is tiny: ahead/behind your target, no lost result after a crash, optional roxzone laps. No new tab bar before the race.
  • Three tabs: Today · Library · Log, with a big + Add button on every tab. Insights becomes a tab once there is enough data.
  • A day is a Session made of Blocks; lifting blocks run from the phone (your decision), metcons on the watch; one Apple workout per active block.
  • Scaling lives on the result; Add gets "Log a result", several screenshots at once and the Share Sheet.
  • Code check: the watch already queues and re-sends results, workouts already save to Apple Health, End already asks "End workout?". Missing: reading back saved progress after a crash.

2Principles

  1. The watch is the source of truth. It measures, saves and keeps the result until the phone has it. A phone in a locker never costs you a workout.
  2. Never lose a result. Not to a crash, a flat battery or a wrong tap. Every fix (splits, scaling, date) can be undone, and the raw recording is never changed.
  3. Hands off mid-workout. Crown turn = next, side button back = undo. No tapping with chalky hands.
  4. Always check before saving. Anything the app read from a photo or text goes through one confirm screen. It never guesses silently.
  5. Only claim what the data supports. Insights say "based on 3 results" and grey out what they can't know yet.
  6. Small, testable steps. Code is written by AI agents without a compiler and tested by you, so each build changes one thing you can check in a real workout.

3Bar path: inside the app (unchanged)

Decision

Bar path stays inside Rx Lab, as the analysis of every lift. Not a tab, not a separate app.

You see it when you open a lift: path, bar speed, pull and catch heights. Each lift type has one Lift page (Library › Lifts) with best lifts, your recent maxes (3RM, 5RM, 10RM) and later speed by load. A Mayhem day with squats and a metcon stays one entry, with heart rate across both. "BarPath analysis, by Rx Lab" stays the feature name.

How sync works today, in plain words: the watch keeps each finished result as a file in an "outbox" and hands it to Apple's watch-to-phone delivery service, which waits until the phone is around. The file is deleted only after the phone has it, and anything left over is sent again next time the watch app opens. New: the watch also tells the phone which files it still holds, so the phone can show "still on the watch". Every new file type (e.g. a lifting block) must be added to the re-send list, with a test.

How phone-controlled lifting works

  • Before opening the watch app, the phone sends the plan for the block (lifts, sets, suggested loads, units, plate steps). The watch app then starts the strength workout by itself. The watch owns the workout: it records heart rate and motion and saves to Apple Health.
  • Apple's live link between watch and phone workouts (built into iPhone iOS 17 and watchOS 10, which Rx Lab already requires) gives the phone a live copy and a channel for short commands: Set done, Next lift, change load, End. We define these commands once, in shared code with tests.
  • The watch keeps the log of what happened and gives each set its ID. Each time the phone (re)connects, the watch sends its full current state, so nothing depends on every message arriving.
  • The phone must be nearby. If it drops out, the watch keeps recording; you can use Next set / Next lift / End on the watch. Reopening the phone app re-attaches to the running workout.
  • End lifting saves the whole lifting block as one Traditional Strength Training workout. Metcon blocks are separate workouts started on the watch.
  • Sets are marked by you (decided rule, see §7): Double Tap on the watch at unrack and at rerack, "Set done" on the phone as backup. Today's motion detector only knows Olympic pulls from the floor; squats and presses get their own detection once enough marked sets are recorded.

7Measuring every barbell lift

You want all barbell lifts measured, not only snatch and clean & jerk: back and front squat, overhead squat, strict press and push press, bench later. For each set: reps, bar speed per rep and the bar path. The hard part is knowing when a set starts and stops. Plan: you mark it by hand now, the marks teach the app, then the app suggests and you confirm.

Why this can work

  • In back and front squats and presses your hands hold the bar, so the watch wrist moves with the bar: wrist motion is a good stand-in for bar motion.
  • On bench the watch arm also moves with the bar.
  • Squats and presses have a clear rhythm (down-up), easier to count than Olympic lifts.

Limits, said honestly

  • Until step 3 ships for a lift, reps come from what you type, not from motion.
  • Overhead squat: the wrist is still, the body moves under it; detection may need more data.
  • Bar speed from a wrist is an estimate; it is shown as one, compared only with your own sets.

8The data model in plain words

Workout (definition)

The plan: blocks, movements, reps, loads in two columns when the source has them (e.g. 185/125 lb; you pick your column once in Profile), cap, instructions, the original text or photo. Editing makes a new version; old results keep pointing at the version they did. "My version of Murph" links to Murph but is never compared as Rx.

Block

One part of a day, with its own type and score: Warm-up (no score) · Strength (load per set, e.g. 5×3 at 65–75%) · Metcon (time, or rounds + reps at the cap) · Skill/Gymnastics (e.g. 100 V-ups, cap 7) · Accessory ("2 rounds for quality", done / not done) · Cool-down · Reference (a note or link, e.g. the scaling guide).

Session

One training day or visit: date, time zone, gym. It holds the results of its blocks. One Session = one row in the Log, even though Apple Health gets one workout per active block (e.g. lifting block, metcon, V-ups test). Planned days carry a session ID and block IDs to the watch, and the watch sends them back with each result, so grouping is exact. Only unplanned workouts are grouped by date (you can split them). Old files become Sessions too: an old lift session = one lifting block, an old workout result = one metcon block.

Lifting block

Run from the phone. Strength blocks that follow each other (back squat, then front squat) run as one lifting block: one recording, one Apple strength workout, but each lift keeps its own result. Start lifting on the first card includes the next ones. Sets are marked by you (Double Tap on the watch, Set done on the phone as backup); each set gets an ID from the watch and its own slice of motion data, which also becomes training data (§7).

Scores for lifts

"Heaviest weight you can for each set" scores the best set (e.g. 3 reps at 245 lb), and that set updates your rep maxes. Total volume (load × reps, added up: Mayhem's "3675 lb" = 5 sets × 3 × 245 lb) is shown as a second number, never as the score.

Result

What you did in one block: score (and its type), Rx / Scaled / Foundations / custom, which "this or that" option you chose, actual loads, team score, notes, RPE (how hard, 1–10), source (watch, typed in, official), "incomplete" flag.

Recording

The raw watch data of one active block (segment times or lift/set markers, heart rate, steps, bar motion). Never changed after the workout.

Edits

Fixes are kept separate from the raw recording: moved splits, trimmed end, scaling, date, and loads/make-miss typed on the phone (linked to the watch's set ID). A result sent again by the watch can't erase them. Each result file has a revision number; the phone keeps the newest. "Restore original" removes the fixes. PRs and trends are recalculated after every edit.

Partner splits

A partner or doubles result keeps the team score. Each split can be tagged me / partner / together. Heart-rate and effort insights only use "me" and "together". Time while your partner works counts as rest.

Maxes and units

Rep maxes per lift (1RM, 3RM, 5RM, 10RM, with dates). "70% 1RM" becomes a suggested load, rounded to your plates. Base: your latest tested 1RM; otherwise the best estimate from a rep max in the last 12 months, shown with its source ("from 3RM 215 lb, Feb 26 · est. 1RM ~230"). Older results are shown greyed ("over a year ago") and not used. Stored in kg, metres and seconds plus the unit you typed, so 95 lb never shows as 43.09 kg.

PRs compare like with like: same workout, same scaling level, same score type. Scaled PRs count too. A capped result reads "82 reps at cap (7:00)" everywhere, never "CAP+82".

9Add, import and log a result

The Add sheet

  • Photo / screenshots: one or several images (a whiteboard, 6 Mayhem cards). Text is read on the iPhone itself, so weak gym Wi-Fi doesn't matter.
  • Paste text: from WhatsApp, Instagram, a programming app.
  • Build: the same confirm screen, starting empty. Formats: For time (+cap) · AMRAP · EMOM / Every N:00 · Rounds with rest · Intervals / Tabata · Strength sets (% or load) · Distance run · HYROX.
  • Log a result: no watch. Pick or describe the workout, enter score, scaling, notes.
  • Start on watch: Free workout or Lift session.
  • From other apps: Share › Rx Lab (iOS Share Sheet) opens the same confirm screen.
Photo · Paste · Share→Split into blocks→Match movements→Pick track→Confirm screen→Save · Schedule · Send to watch

The confirm screen

  • Your original image or text sits on top; you can always look back at it.
  • The day is shown as blocks, each with its type and score type (you can change both, merge or split blocks).
  • Each line shows what it was matched to: "T2B → Toes-to-bar". Unsure lines get a ⚑ flag and the word "check"; unknown movements stay as free text and still show on the watch.
  • Tracks: if the source has Rx / Scaled / Foundations, you pick one. Two-column loads ("185/125") use the column you chose in Profile; until you choose, the screen asks.
  • "%1RM" lines show the suggested loads from your maxes, with the source. A "target score" in the instructions is kept and can later be your ahead/behind target.
  • One tap at the end: Save, or Save and send to watch.

  1. What Rx Lab does, in one sentence. Watch check: "Rx Lab is on your watch ✓" or how to install it.

Empty states (one action each)

  • Today: "Nothing planned. [+ Add today's workout] or start a Free workout on your watch."
  • Log: "Your first workout shows up here a minute after you press End."
  • Library › Mine: "Import your box's whiteboard or a Mayhem day."

Watch app crashes or battery dies mid-race S-55 · S-47 Before Anaheim

  1. At each Crown turn the watch has written its progress (with the workout's ID) to a file.
  2. watchOS restarts the app and hands back the running Apple workout (Apple's workout recovery). Screen: "Hyrox · 41:12 · run 5 · [Resume] [Save as incomplete]" (illustrative).
  3. Resume: the same Apple workout continues, the clock runs from the real start time, splits kept; heart rate during the gap is filled from Apple Health. Save: the phone shows the report marked "Incomplete" with "Complete manually".
  4. If the battery died, there is nothing to resume: next time the watch is charged and opened, Save as incomplete keeps everything up to the last Crown turn.
  5. Rehearsed before the race with Advanced › "Simulate crash".

Note · added 1 Nov 2026

Transition time vs work time in every segment

The owner wants transitions measured. A prototype (repo: docs/TRANSITIONS.md, tools/barpath/transitions.py) splits each segment into transition (from the Crown turn to the first real rep, stroke or running step) and work, from the motion recording, with no extra Crown turns. On AMRAP 40 (1 Nov) it found the start of work in 28 of 28 stations: 7:38 of 33:54 (23 %) went to transitions.

  • Data model: each split gets optional transition seconds + confidence, computed after the workout from the recording (needs DATA on). Never edits the raw times.
  • Workout report: lighter leading part on each split bar (solid bar when confidence is low); one line under the score: "Time in transitions 7:38 (23 %)".
  • HYROX: the transition before each station and run ≈ Roxzone time; ties in with the optional Roxzone laps.
  • Insights (later): transitions per movement over time ("you lose 30–40 s setting up wall walks").
  • Build stage: after 2–3 more workouts with DATA on and a short phone video of a few stations to validate; not before Anaheim.
