# BarPath: Apple Watch bar-path tracker

Start a session on the watch, walk to the bar, lift, drop it. A few seconds later the
iPhone (sitting on a bench with the BarPath app open) shows that lift's bar path, velocity
curve and metrics. The next lift replaces it on screen, and earlier lifts stay in the list
for the session. Only the lifts are stored, not the walking in between. A debug mode also
records the whole session as a CSV so the lift detector can be tuned on real data.

The binding contract is `../docs/RECORDER_SPEC.md`.

```
BarPath/
  project.yml        XcodeGen spec (BarPath.xcodeproj is generated, not committed)
  BarPathKit/        Swift package: data format, detector, analyzer (+ tests)
  iOS/               iPhone app (target "BarPath", com.slimslim.barpath)
  Watch/             Watch app (target "BarPathWatch", com.slimslim.barpath.watchkitapp)
```

---

## 1. One-time setup on your Mac

1. Install **Xcode** (16 or newer) from the App Store and open it once so it installs its
   components. Sign in under *Xcode > Settings > Accounts* with your Apple ID (a free account
   works for running on your own devices; HealthKit works with free provisioning too).
2. Install **Homebrew** (https://brew.sh) if you don't have it, then XcodeGen:
   ```sh
   brew install xcodegen
   ```
3. Generate and open the project:
   ```sh
   cd BarPath
   xcodegen
   open BarPath.xcodeproj
   ```
   Re-run `xcodegen` whenever files are added or `project.yml` changes. Any change made in
   Xcode's project settings is lost on regeneration, so put settings in `project.yml`.
4. **Set your team.** Easiest and permanent: open `project.yml`, set
   `DEVELOPMENT_TEAM: "AB12CD34EF"` (your 10-character Team ID from *Xcode > Settings >
   Accounts* or developer.apple.com > Membership) and run `xcodegen` again.
   Alternative: in Xcode select the project > target **BarPath** > *Signing & Capabilities* >
   Team, then the same for target **BarPathWatch**. Both targets must use the same team.
   - If Xcode says the bundle id is taken (free accounts), change `com.slimslim.barpath`
     in `project.yml` to something unique, change the watch id to `<new id>.watchkitapp`, and
     update `WKCompanionAppBundleIdentifier` in `Watch/Info.plist` to the new iPhone id.
5. **Developer Mode** (required to run your own builds):
   - iPhone: connect it by cable once, then *Settings > Privacy & Security > Developer Mode*
     > On, restart, confirm.
   - Apple Watch: with the iPhone connected and the watch paired, open Xcode's
     *Window > Devices and Simulators* so Xcode sees the watch, then on the watch
     *Settings > Privacy & Security > Developer Mode* > On, restart, confirm.
     Keep the watch on its charger, unlocked, near the Mac/iPhone the first time; preparing
     a watch for development can take several minutes.

## 2. Build and run

- Scheme **BarPathWatch**, destination your **Apple Watch Series 12** (shown via your
  iPhone) > Run (⌘R). This installs the watch app and also builds the iPhone app that
  contains it.
- Scheme **BarPath**, destination your **iPhone** > Run. Do this at least once so the iPhone
  app is installed (it embeds the watch app, so installing it from the phone also works).
- On first start the watch asks for Motion and Health access. The Health prompt is approved
  **on the iPhone**: unlock the phone and open BarPath (or the Health notification) to accept.
- Check in Xcode: target *BarPath* > *Build Phases* should contain **Embed Watch Content**
  with `BarPathWatch.app`. XcodeGen creates it because the iOS app depends on the watchOS
  application target (Xcode 14+ single-target watch app). If it is missing, update XcodeGen
  (`brew upgrade xcodegen`) and regenerate.

## 4. Recording lifts

1. Wear the watch **snug on the back of the wrist** (face on the back of the hand side, as
   normal). Loose straps blur the data. Same wrist every time.
2. Open the **BarPath app on the iPhone** and leave it on the **Live** tab, on the bench,
   screen on (the app keeps the screen awake while Live is open).
3. On the watch: pick the lift type (and load with the Digital Crown if you like), optionally
   turn on **Record full session (debug)**, tap **Start**.
4. Walk to the bar. Get into your set position and **stay still for about 1 second** (the
   watch shows "Ready" and taps your wrist).
5. Lift. **Hold the finish still for about 1 second** (receiving position / overhead).
6. Drop or lower the bar. About 2 to 5 seconds later the lift appears on the phone.
7. Repeat for the next lift; each new lift becomes the big card, earlier ones move to the
   list below. Change the lift type on the watch between lifts if needed.
8. Tap **End** on the watch when done. With debug recording on, the session CSV is sent then.

Reading the screen: the bar-path chart is a side view (x = forward, + = away from you;
y = height above the start, both in cm, same scale). Dot color = speed. Markers: Start, Max
(highest point before the catch) and Catch. The faint grey line is the previous lift. The
velocity chart shows vertical speed over time; dashed lines are still points the analyzer
used to cancel drift. Confidence and the analyzer's notes are shown as-is; take low
confidence lifts with a grain of salt.

## 5. Home test protocol (data for tuning the detector)

No barbell needed: a broomstick or PVC pipe (or an empty bar) is enough.

1. On the watch turn on **Record full session (debug)**, choose *Snatch*, tap **Start**.
   Keep the iPhone app open.
2. Do **5 slow mock snatches**. For each rep:
   - walk around a bit first (a few steps, arms moving normally);
   - tap **Mark** on the watch right before the rep;
   - set position, **still ~1 s**; lift; hold the finish **still ~1 s**;
   - lower or drop the stick, then walk around again.
3. Switch the lift type to *Clean & Jerk* on the watch and do **5 mock clean & jerks** the
   same way (still ~1 s in the set, in the front rack if you pause there, and overhead).
4. Tap **End**. Wait until the phone's **Debug** tab lists the new recording (pull to
   refresh).
5. Export (section 6) the session CSV and the lift files of that session and send them over.
   Note anything odd (missed reps, extra lifts) and roughly when.

## 6. Exporting recordings

- **Debug tab**: every full-session CSV with date, duration, size; share button per file
  and **Share all recordings**. Tap a recording to count your Mark taps and re-run the lift
  detector on the phone.
- **Lift files**: share one lift from its detail screen (share icon), a whole session from
  *History > session > Share*, or everything from *Debug > Share all lift files*.
- Share via **AirDrop** to the Mac, or *Save to Files*. All files are also visible in the
  Files app under *On My iPhone > BarPath* and in Finder (iPhone > Files) when connected.
  Layout: `Documents/Lifts/<sessionId>/<liftId>.json`, `Documents/Debug/session-<sessionId>.csv`.

## 7. Troubleshooting

- **Lifts don't show up on the phone.** Transfers are delivered only while the iPhone app
  is running (or shortly after it was open). Keep BarPath open on the Live tab with the
  screen on; if the phone locked, open the app and queued lifts arrive within seconds.
  The watch keeps unsent files and retries, so nothing is lost by waiting.
- **"Watch app missing" / "No watch paired"** in the Live tab chip: install the watch app
  (run the BarPathWatch scheme, or the Watch app on the iPhone > BarPath > Install).
- **Health permission**: the watch's Health request must be approved on the iPhone. Unlock
  the phone, open BarPath, and accept the Health sheet. You can change it later in
  *Settings > Health > Data Access & Devices > BarPath*.
- **No bar path for a lift** ("couldn't find a still set position"): hold the set position
  still for a full second before pulling.
- **Path drifts / low confidence**: hold the finish still for ~1 s before dropping, and
  tighten the strap.
- **Signing errors**: both targets need the same team; bundle ids must be unique for free
  accounts (see setup step 4).
- **Debug CSV missing**: it is sent only after **End**, and can be ~20 MB per 10 min, so give
  it a minute with the iPhone app open.

Known limitations (v1): the watch is on the wrist, not the bar (a few cm of offset); the
forward direction assumes the watch is on the back of the wrist; the horizontal path is
less reliable than the vertical; standing up from the set without pulling can produce a
small false lift.
